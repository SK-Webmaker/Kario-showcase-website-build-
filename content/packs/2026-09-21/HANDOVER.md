One standalone Reel. **27 seconds, 1080×1920, bright theme.** Caption passed the

## What it is

The simplest possible explanation of Kairo, built for someone who has never
heard of it and is not really paying attention.

**Six jobs → six tools → one app.** The whole film is one idea:

1. **"A salon does six jobs a day."** Six tiles arrive, one at a time — take
   bookings, remember clients, take the money, send reminders, fill the gaps,
   never double-book.
2. **"Most salons do them in six different places."** The same six tiles scatter
   into disorder and turn orange, and their labels flip to what a salon actually
   uses instead: a paper diary, a spreadsheet, a card machine, texting by hand,
   hoping, memory.
3. **"Kairo is one."** The tiles get dragged back to the centre, shrink, and a
   single blue card takes their place.

That collapse is the whole video. Everything else is labelling. It is the bit
worth watching twice, which is the bit that makes something get sent to someone.

Then four seconds of the real dashboard so it is not just a cartoon, and the
price.

## The colour change you asked for

Warm cream ground, near-black ink, a deeper Kairo blue, and one orange used only
on the mess and never on the product.

**Two things I did deliberately, and you should know about both:**

- **Kairo's blue is still the only accent doing any work** — just one step
  deeper (`#2563EB`), because the product's `#3b82f6` is too light to hold on
  cream. So it reads as the same company in a different mood, rather than a
  different company. If you would rather it were a full break from the brand,
  say so and I will take the blue out entirely.
- **This is one asset, not a new house style.** It is recorded in the working
  memory as a decision rather than left to look like drift.

Cream also does something useful: almost every Reel in a salon owner's feed is
dark or is footage. A bright, flat, high-contrast frame is the thing the thumb
catches.

## An honest caveat on measuring it

**Two variables moved at once here** — the message got radically simpler *and*
the colour changed. So if this outperforms the dark Reels, I will not be able to
tell you which one did it. That is the cost of the request, and it is worth
saying rather than quietly claiming a win for the colour later.

If you want that untangled, the next step is a dark-theme version of this exact
script. Same words, same timing, old palette. Then it is a clean comparison.
